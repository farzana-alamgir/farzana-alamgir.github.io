### Papers Under Review

##### Oil Price Uncertainty and Sovereign Spreads of Oil Importers
*Revise and Resubmit, Journal of Economic Dynamics and Control* · [Latest version](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1t5myHFw2vMuoT16lEA3se_37_riLG0wY/view?usp=drive_link)

<details>
<summary><strong>Abstract</strong></summary>
<p>This paper studies how changes in the volatility of global oil prices affect sovereign spreads. First, I establish a positive correlation between sovereign spreads and oil price volatility for 35 oil-importing countries. I then introduce time-varying volatility in the global oil price within a sovereign default model with long-duration bonds for a small open economy that imports oil as a factor of production. I model oil prices as a stochastic volatility process, estimated using Bayesian Markov Chain Monte Carlo techniques. My findings indicate that during highly volatile periods, the response of sovereign spreads to fluctuations in oil price levels is three times more pronounced than in less volatile times, and that higher debt levels intensify the sensitivity of spreads to changes in the global oil price. Because rising oil prices raise spreads much more when volatility is high, the government borrows proactively when it anticipates future oil price increases. This proactive borrowing serves as insurance against higher import costs and borrowing rates if oil prices surge.</p>
</details>
